About the project

UAV SILM started as our STEAM project at Gustav Adolfi Gümnaasium. SILM Buteo is the aircraft it produced; this part of the site covers the project itself: the subjects behind it, the team, our progress and the build. The presentation is on 8 December 2026.

Physics
Hover or tap

Aerodynamics: lift and drag in XFLR5 and OpenFOAM, balance, flight dynamics, and the thrust rig's lever-arm mechanics.

Mathematics
Hover or tap

Centre-of-gravity and static-margin calculations from the 3D model, tail volume sizing, load factors in turns, and the thrust conversion formula.

Materials science
Hover or tap

Lightweight foaming LW-PLA for skins, PETG for loaded parts, trays and gaskets, carbon-fibre spars, epoxy and carbon-filament rivets.

IT
Hover or tap

ArduPilot configuration, map-based mission planning, an encrypted WireGuard link, Python on a Raspberry Pi for the thrust rig, and the 3D viewers on this site.

Civics and defence
Hover or tap

Estonian and European defence capability, and the ethics and regulation of dual-use technology.

Project management

"Fixed-Wing UAV Design: A Systems Engineering Approach" (ResearchGate)

What we took: a systems-level design process, which helped us order the stages (aerodynamics, structure, electronics, testing).

ScienceDirect article on fixed-wing UAVs

What we took: background on aerodynamic approaches and airfoil selection.

"Development of a Hand-Launched Small UAV for Ground Reconnaissance" (ResearchGate)

What we took: design principles for small hand-launched reconnaissance aircraft, the model for our launch method.

UAS Components (uascomponents.com)

What we took: a reference for component types and specifications.

IRE Journal (irejournals.com)

What we took: background on UAV applications.

Professional encrypted flight-controller literature (AES-256 link systems)

What we took: the idea of an encrypted failsafe link, which we adapted into a cheaper 4G LTE and WireGuard concept.

GitHub project "Drone-Based Reconnaissance of Military Assets"

What we took: a comparison point for software and data handling in a similar open-source project.

V-tail sizing references: Flite Test forum on tail volume coefficients, FMSG Alling "V-Leitwerke", Charles River RC "Quick V-tail sizing"

What we took: V-tail area from tail volume coefficients and the 1/cos dihedral correction, which gave our 140 / 90 mm, 35° V-tail.

XFLR5 documentation and guidelines

What we took: how to set up VLM analyses without the fuselage body, and how to estimate inertia from surface and point masses.

OpenFOAM documentation

What we took: setting up the airframe CFD cases, the force coefficients and the streamline sampling.

Mateksys H743-WING and F765-WING manuals

What we took: pinouts, UART assignments and ArduPilot battery-sensing parameters for the wiring diagram.

ArduPilot Plane documentation

What we took: flight-controller setup and waypoint mission planning.

Vernier godirect and RPLCD Python libraries

What we took: reading the force sensor and driving the LCD on the thrust rig.

three.js (MIT licence) new

What we used it for: the interactive 3D viewers on this site, served from our own domain.

Google Open Images V6/V7 (CC BY 4.0; photos CC BY 2.0) new

What we used: about 1,800 labelled photos of tractors, cars, trucks and livestock to train our ready-made recognition model. Each photo belongs to its Flickr author.

Drone pictures of cattle and sheep, Roboflow Universe new

What we used: "aerial-cattle-detection" by cattle-psyty and "counting-cattle" by arthurmessias (CC BY 4.0), and "aerial-sheep" by riis (Public Domain), to teach our recognition model what livestock look like from above.

Ultralytics YOLO11 (AGPL-3.0) new

What we used: the YOLO11n network and training tools; our model is fine-tuned from it and shared under the same licence.

ArduPilot SITL simulator and LiteRT new

What we used: flying our missions with GPS jamming in simulation, and running the recognition model on the Raspberry Pi.

Leaflet, OpenStreetMap and Esri World Imagery new

What we used: the maps in our Ground Station program (map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, imagery © Esri and its data providers).

Claude (Anthropic)

What we used it for: help organising the website content, writing the page's HTML, CSS and JavaScript including the 3D viewers, setting up and running the OpenFOAM CFD of our V4 airframe, and the centre-of-gravity, fit-check and layout calculations.

UAV SILM is an ongoing student STEAM project, not a finished aircraft. Every figure on this page (mass, endurance, cost, balance, control response) is a theoretical estimate from CFD analysis and hand calculation, not from flight-test data. The 3D models show our CAD and simulation results, not a built aircraft.

We have not held formal discussions with Kaitseliit or the Estonian Defence Forces; we present the platform as a design study and a longer-term aim toward a working, versatile aircraft.
