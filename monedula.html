SILM Monedula

A hand-launched observation drone you print, fly and repair yourself. Long endurance, a stabilised camera and an encrypted 4G link, for about €870 in parts. Monedula is the Latin name of the jackdaw.

Monedula

Why Monedula

01
Stays up
An efficient 1.5 m wing (lift-to-drag about 17 in CFD) and one 7000 mAh battery, aiming at about 100 minutes per flight.
02
Sees clearly
Siyi A2 mini camera on a stabilised pitch gimbal under a clear dome in the nose.
03
Flies itself
ArduPilot runs the mission from launch to an automatic landing into the wind.
04
Secure link
Telemetry over 4G inside an encrypted WireGuard tunnel, plus an ELRS receiver for control in the field.
05
Fix it in the field
Every part prints on a 220 mm printer. Break one, print another overnight; the landing skids screw off.
06
No runway
Throw it by hand, land on the belly. The wings slide off with both hatches closed.

Studio renders of the V11c CAD. The propeller is a stand-in.

Specifications

Specifications

Wingspan / length1.50 m / 0.92 m
Take-off massabout 1.8 kg
Cruise / stall speedabout 15 m/s (54 km/h) / about 10 m/s
Enduranceabout 100 min target
CameraSiyi A2 mini, 160° wide, tilts −90° to +25°
AutopilotArduPilot on a Matek H743, Raspberry Pi 4 for recognition
Link4G LTE with WireGuard encryption; ELRS 2.4 GHz receiver for field control
Power4S 7000 mAh, T-Motor AT2321 pusher inside the tail
Launch / landingHand launch / belly on replaceable skids
Airframe70 printed parts in LW-PLA, PETG and TPU on a 10 mm carbon tube

Monedula is designed and CFD-checked but not built yet. All figures are design estimates, not flight data.
