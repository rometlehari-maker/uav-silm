SILM Monedula

Monedula is our plan for the successor to SILM Buteo. It keeps the same electronics, camera and software, and reworks the airframe around everything we learnt from Buteo: a slimmer fuselage, a longer wing on a stronger spar, the motor outside the tail, and a belly hardpoint for heavier payloads. Monedula is the Latin name of the jackdaw.

What changes from SILM Buteo

AreaSILM ButeoMonedula
FuselageAbout 98 mm round, components spread out79 x 81 mm, electronics on two removable racks behind the battery
Wing1.4 m, 3° dihedral, 7 mm carbon tube1.5 m, flat, 10 mm carbon tube with 6 mm rods outboard; bolts on with two screws
MotorInside the tail coneOutside on a PETG end plug, in free air for cooling
TailV-tail close to the propellerV-tail moved forward and 8 % larger, 70 mm clear of the propeller
PayloadCamera in the noseSwappable nose module on four heat-set inserts, plus a 1 kg hardpoint under the balance point
Design methodHand-modelled in SolidWorksGenerated from parametric Python code, so a dimension change rebuilds and rechecks every part

Calculated with a quick aerodynamic method: stall about 9.8 m/s empty, static margin about 14 %. These are design estimates only; Monedula has not had a full CFD run yet.

Steps before it flies

1

Check the CAD

Open every part in SolidWorks and confirm the parts fit together as modelled.

2

Print test pieces

Print one fuselage joint, the nose and a wing root, fit the real camera and spar, and load-test the joint.

3

Run CFD

Run OpenFOAM on the new shape to replace the quick estimates for drag, stability and endurance.

4

Build and weigh

Print the full airframe, weigh every part and correct the balance before the first flight.
