Lessons

What we learnt, and what went wrong

The analysis caught several problems before anything was printed, while they were still cheap to fix. These are the lessons we are taking into the build, and the troubles that taught them.

Weigh the airframe from the CAD

Our first balance sheet used placeholder masses for the fuselage, wings and V-tail. Taking volumes from the 3D model gave a 574 g airframe with its own CG at 435 mm, and put the whole aircraft's CG at 411 mm: behind the neutral point, so it would have been unstable.

Moving the wing beats adding weight

Moving the wing 20 mm aft shifted the neutral point 18.2 mm but the CG only about 5 mm. It did the job of about 80 g of nose weight, for no extra mass.

Balance and trim speed move together

Moving the CG aft lowers the stability margin and the trim speed at the same time: 21.2 m/s with the CG at 335 mm, 18.8 m/s at 345 mm. The tail incidence sets the rest.

Check the fit in cross-section

In side view everything fitted. Slicing the round fuselage showed three clashes, and that the battery can only sit on the centreline.

Each tool sees a different aircraft

XFLR5 models the wing and tail with partly laminar flow; OpenFOAM adds the fuselage and assumes turbulent flow everywhere. Lift agreed within 5%, while drag at 3° came out about 60% higher in CFD. Knowing what each tool includes tells us which number to use for what.

Heat is a design load for printed parts

LW-PLA softens at around 55 to 60 °C. The motor sits inside the tail cone and the ESC near the floor, so we are adding vents and printing trays and loaded parts in PETG.

Some choices are mechanical, not about balance

Putting the tail servos at x 520 or x 740 changes the CG by only 2.4 mm, so we will choose between them on pushrod stiffness, access and heat.

Our first CG estimate would have made the aircraft unstable

The CG came out at 411 mm, 47 mm behind the neutral point.

What we didRebuilt the mass model from CAD volumes, moved the electronics forward, planned a lighter V-tail and moved the wing 20 mm aft. The static margin is now about 20%.

Three components did not fit

The Raspberry Pi, the camera and the motor all clashed with the round fuselage or the narrowing tail cone.

What we didPi to the upper tray, camera behind a nose window, motor 19 mm further forward on its long shaft.

The CAD would not mesh directly for CFD

Some curved surfaces in the STEP export failed in the first meshing tool.

What we didFused the parts and triangulated them another way before snappyHexMesh could use the geometry.

Parallel CFD runs failed at first

Running the solver on two cores clashed with the installed MPI library.

What we didA library workaround, after which each case ran in parallel to convergence.

CFD drag disagreed with XFLR5

0.0367 against 0.0227 at 3°.

What we didTraced it to the fuselage and the fully turbulent boundary layer in CFD. We treat the CFD drag as an upper estimate until flight tests.

Our first failsafe settings assumed a radio we do not have

They were written for a pilot with an ExpressLRS radio who would land by hand. Without one, a radio failsafe would trigger at once, the aircraft would refuse to arm, and a return home would end in circling until the battery ran out. Several parameter names did not exist in ArduPlane at all.

What we didWrote a script that checks every parameter against the ArduPlane source, rebuilt the settings for hand launch and automatic landing with no radio, made every failsafe end in that landing, and wrote our own laptop mission planner that always adds the landing.

The camera speed estimate had its sign reversed

A test against an independent camera model showed forward flight reading as flying backwards. The estimate was switched off by default, so it never reached the autopilot.

What we didFixed the signs, added correction for pitch and roll, and now test it on rendered images of the ground.

The build started late

Airframe printing has not started; the print settings for the foaming LW-PLA still need tuning.

What we didPlanned test prints first (LW-PLA from 5 November, PETG from 13 November) and replaced the stage plan with dated tasks. See the progress report.

The second parts order slipped

Carbon tube sizes, wire gauge and the arming connector had to come from the CAD first.

What we didFinished the sizes and set the order for 4 November.
  • Build the mass model from the CAD on day one, and update it whenever a part changes.
  • Fit-check components in cross-section before choosing their positions.
  • Order the secondary parts together with the primary set.
  • Run a mesh-independence study before trusting drag values.
  • Leave room for cooling in every closed compartment.

✓

CFD caught a real problem before printing

Running OpenFOAM on Monedula showed it would have been unstable in pitch. We found the cause, changed the design and confirmed the fix with a second run, all before printing anything.

✓

The design is now fully parametric

Monedula is a parametric FreeCAD model. Lengthening the fuselage moved the wing, bay, tail and every joint together, and the clash checks ran again automatically on all 69 parts.

✓

Problems were found on paper

The balance and fit checks found an unstable CG and three component clashes before any part was printed, while they were still cheap to fix.

✓

Main components arrived on schedule

We planned procurement for 6 to 19 October and had all eight primary components in hand on 20 October, so the electronics work is no longer waiting on deliveries.

✓

The aerodynamic design is complete

XFLR5 and OpenFOAM agree on lift and stability, and with the wing moved aft the aircraft has a static margin of about 20%. Aerodynamics is no longer holding up the build.

✓

CAD reached a full-aircraft assembly

Version 4 brings the fuselage, wing, hollow tail with connectors and nose mounts together in one model, which we now use for sectioning, part design and the balance calculation.

✓

We built our own thrust rig

Rather than relying on manufacturer data, we designed and built a stand that measures static thrust for any motor and propeller combination. It will let us check the propulsion choice before flight.

✓

Clear split of work

A shared task sheet with priorities and owners, and a dated plan to 8 December, means each of us knows what is due and when.

!

Our design tools disagreed

The quick aerodynamic tool we used to size Monedula gave a 14 % static margin; CFD gave −3.4 %. We now check stability with a method that includes downwash and confirm it in CFD.

!

Joints and hinges are still unproven

The PETG couplers, root ribs and TPU hinges have only been checked in CAD. Bond strength and hinge life need printed test pieces before we trust them in flight.

!

The build phase started late

Airframe printing has not started yet. LW-PLA is a foaming filament whose weight and strength depend strongly on temperature and flow, so we need test prints to dial it in (5 to 10 November), followed by PETG settings (13 to 16 November).

!

The second component order slipped

The secondary list has been open since 20 October, and several items still needed sizing from the CAD: carbon tube sizes, wire gauge and the battery arming connector. We will order on 4 November.

!

Joining printed sections

The wing and fuselage are printed in sections joined with overlapping edges, carbon-filament rivets, bolts and PETG root gaskets. Hole tolerances have to be proven with test pieces before we commit to full parts.

!

One structural decision is still open

We have not yet decided whether the 12 mm main spar joins at mid-fuselage or runs the full span. It affects transport, assembly and wing removal.

!

Software work is behind the hardware

Flight-controller programming and datalink research have not started. The internal layout is now fit-checked, but the trays and mounts still need to be modelled. These now sit on the critical path for ground testing.

!

The thrust rig took longer than expected

We sketched it up to version 5 before building, and it is still being finalised. It has not yet been run with a battery.

Design log

Design log

Every design change, what triggered it and what it achieved. Newest at the bottom.

  1. 3 Nov
    Buteo

    First CFD of the full aircraft. Lift matched XFLR5, but the neutral point came out further forward. We moved the wing 20 mm aft: static margin from 13% to 20%, trim speed close to the 20 m/s target, no weight added.

  2. 10 Nov
    Buteo

    V9 parts: joints, battery tray, servo caps, the 590 g battery, Raspberry Pi 4 and 7 mm wing tube. Calculated all-up mass 1.70 kg.

  3. 16 Nov
    Monedula

    First CFD of V10b. Lift-to-drag about 18 near 2°, but static margin −3.4%: statically unstable. Our design tool had predicted +14% because it ignores the wing's downwash on the tail; a vortex-lattice check agreed with the CFD.

  4. 17 Nov
    Monedula

    V11: fuselage stretched 68 mm behind the battery, so the wing, bay, tail and motor move aft; V-tail span +7%. CFD static margin 13.7%. Moving only the wing would have put the spar through the Raspberry Pi, and a bigger tail alone reached only 4.6%. The central payload bay was dropped; payloads go on the modular nose.

  5. 17–18 Nov
    Monedula

    V11b: smoothed fuselage creases, re-attached the V-tail root, put the ruddervator hinges on the twisted mean line, stiffened the tail tips and moved the motor inside the tail end on a PETG mount that slides out aft.

  6. 19 Nov
    Monedula

    V11c: an audit of every joint, screw, insert and insertion path in exact CAD geometry. Fixed aileron clearance (now ±30°), wings that would not slide on with a hatch closed, a battery that could not be inserted, two couplers that could not be fitted, and screws that bottomed out. Added the ELRS receiver inside the F3 wall.

  7. 19 Nov
    Studies

    Rejected: a Bayraktar-style tail boom (+9 g for −0.9% drag) and an all-PETG version for recycled bottles (about 2.3 kg, endurance about 73% of V11b).

  8. 23 Nov
    Buteo

    Printed parts recalculated at 600 g; the mass model had 567 g. All-up mass 1.73 kg, CG 343.7 mm, static margin 19.5%, trim speed 20.1 m/s. Still well inside the stable range.

UAV SILM is an ongoing student STEAM project, not a finished aircraft. Every figure on this page (mass, endurance, cost, balance, control response) is a theoretical estimate from CFD analysis and hand calculation, not from flight-test data. The 3D models show our CAD and simulation results, not a built aircraft.

We have not held formal discussions with Kaitseliit or the Estonian Defence Forces; we present the platform as a design study and a longer-term aim toward a working, versatile aircraft.
