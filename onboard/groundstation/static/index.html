Set up your aircraft

Ten short steps, about an hour the first time. Each step checks itself: when it turns green, go on to the next. You only do this once per aircraft.

    1 · Start

    Welcome

    First, set up your aircraft
    A guided setup checks every part for you, one step at a time.

    Plan a fully automatic survey flight, send it to the aircraft, launch it, and look at the pictures it took. Work through the steps on the left from top to bottom. Everything is saved on this laptop as you go.

    Your progress

      Missions on this laptop

      Open a saved mission, or start a new one (it copies the aircraft answers from the current mission).

        How a flight works

        1. Aircraft - answer the questions about your aircraft once.
        2. Mission - draw the area on the map, set where you launch and land, enter today's wind.
        3. Camera - choose what it should look for.
        4. Send to aircraft - USB cable to the flight controller; Wi-Fi or a USB stick to the aircraft's computer.
        5. Launch - tick the checklist, arm, throw. It flies, lands and switches itself off.
        6. Flights & pictures - bring the pictures back and see where each one was taken.

        Before the first real flight, fly your mission in the Simulator (step 7).

        5 · Send to aircraft

        Send to the aircraft

        Two parts of the aircraft get your answers: the flight controller (flies the aircraft; USB cable) and the aircraft computer (the Raspberry Pi that takes the pictures; its own Wi-Fi, or a USB stick). Do both after every change. The aircraft must be disarmed.

        A · Flight controller (USB cable)

        Which USB port is the flight controller plugged into?

        Plug the USB cable into the flight controller and the battery in. Windows shows it as COM3, COM4…; Linux as /dev/ttyACM0.

        After steps 1-2 the first time, unplug the battery and USB for 10 s (reboot) so the flight controller starts the script.

        Or use Mission Planner / QGroundControl instead

        Download the files and load them there (parameters: CONFIG > Full Parameter List > Load from file; mission: PLAN > Load).

                  Aircraft computer (Raspberry Pi)
                  
      
                  
        
                  Install the aircraft computer (first time)
                  
        
                  For a new or rewritten memory card. Write the card with Raspberry Pi
          Imager first (Raspberry Pi OS Lite 64-bit; in its settings: hostname silm,
          a username and password, your home Wi-Fi and country, SSH on), start the Pi and wait two
          minutes; this laptop on the same Wi-Fi. Then this installs everything over the network.
          Running it again on an installed Pi is safe (it updates it).
                  
      
                  
      
                  
        
                  Check everything
                  
        
                  Tests the camera, gimbal, the link to the flight controller (GPS, failsafe
          settings, GPS-loss script, the mission on board), the recognition speed against your
          height and speed, storage, temperature and clock. About a minute; the Pi stops taking
          pictures meanwhile.

                    Calibrate the sensors
                    
        
                    Do these once on a new aircraft (and the compass again after moving the GPS or
          changing wiring). Each step tells you what to do with the aircraft.
                    
        
                    
          
                    Accelerometers
                    You put the aircraft in 6 positions (level, on each side, nose down, nose up, upside down). Most important.
                    
          
                    Level
                    Sit the aircraft on a table in its flying attitude (wings level, nose at its cruise angle). It remembers that as "level".
                    
          
                    Compass
                    Outdoors or away from metal: turn the aircraft through every direction until the bar is full.
                    
          
                    Airspeed sensor
                    Cover the pitot tube with a cup or your fingers (no wind on it), then start. Also zeroes the barometer.
